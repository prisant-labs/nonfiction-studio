# Project Init - Guided

How to use this file: each question has (1) why it matters, (2) how to answer well,
(3) an example. Replace the "Your answer:" line. Be concrete; specifics here save
you re-work in every chapter later.

This intake takes approximately 45-90 minutes. The goal of the first session is a
confirmed brief, not a drafted chapter.

============================================================
SECTION 1 - PROJECT BASICS
============================================================

1.1 Working title (and any subtitle ideas)
    Why it matters: a working title focuses the book. The subtitle usually carries
      the promise for non-fiction.
    How to answer well: give your best current title; add 2 or 3 subtitle options.
      It will change - that is fine.
    Example: "The Quiet Hour - How Morning Routines Rewire Ambition"
    Your answer:

1.2 Genre / subgenre
    Why it matters: it sets structural norms, citation expectations, and tone.
    How to answer well: name the shelf. Options include memoir, narrative
      non-fiction, business/leadership, self-help/personal development, history,
      popular science, how-to/practical, biography, essay collection, journalism/
      long-form, academic-adjacent/crossover.
    Example: "Practical self-help with narrative case studies."

1.3 One-line pitch (the elevator line)
    Why it matters: if you cannot say it in a sentence, the book is not focused yet.
    How to answer well: "This book helps [reader] achieve [outcome] by [approach]."
    Example: "This book helps overwhelmed managers reclaim focus by redesigning
      the first hour of their day."

SECTION 2 - THE BIG IDEA (THESIS)
============================================================

2.1 Controlling idea in one sentence
    Why it matters: this single sentence governs every chapter. Everything must
      serve it or be cut.
    How to answer well: make an assertion, not a topic. "Morning routines matter"
      is a topic. "The first hour of your day sets a ceiling on the rest of it"
      is a thesis.
    Example: "Ambition is not a trait; it is a practice you rebuild every morning."
    Your answer:

2.2 The promise to the reader
    Why it matters: non-fiction is a transaction - the reader gives time, you
      deliver a payoff.
    How to answer well: finish "By the end of this book, you will be able to..."
      or "...you will understand...".
    Example: "...design a morning practice that compounds your focus over months."
    Your answer:

2.3 What this book is NOT
    Why it matters: naming the boundary prevents scope creep, the number one killer
      of non-fiction drafts.
    Example: "Not a productivity-hacks listicle; not a neuroscience textbook."
    Your answer:

      prose, even if you edited it heavily afterward. Knowing this now sets up
      the correct logging from the start.
    How to answer well: answer "yes" if you used or plan to use AI to write any

9.4b AI-assisted content
    Why it matters: assistance (research, outlining, editing suggestions, structural
      recommendations) is treated differently from generation by most disclosure
      standards, including KDP. Both are tracked separately.
    How to answer well: answer "yes" if AI helped or will help you research, outline,
      edit, or structure the book without writing prose that appears in the final text.
    Answer (yes / no / unsure):

9.4c Disclosure stance
    Why it matters: some publishers and platforms, including KDP, require disclosure
      when AI generated or substantially assisted in producing the text.
    Options:

                and in platform metadata

                in your records
