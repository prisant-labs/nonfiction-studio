# The First Question

The library director kept a spreadsheet of attendance for nine years. Every evening class had a
row, and every row had a number. When the county asked her to justify the program, she sent the
spreadsheet, and the county renewed the budget without reading it.

She told me that the renewal felt like a small defeat. The numbers had protected the program, but
they had not described it. A class of six people who stayed for two hours looked smaller on paper
than a class of thirty who left at the break.

So she started asking a different question at the door. She did not ask whether people had
enjoyed the class. She asked what they planned to do the next day that they would not have done
otherwise. The answers were short, often a single sentence, and she wrote each one on a card.

After a month she had two hundred cards. She sorted them on her kitchen table into piles that
made sense to her, and the piles told her more than nine years of rows.
