# Outline

## Argument arc
The book moves the reader from passive observer to active participant in a personal learning network. Each chapter adds one layer of the practice: beginning with the mindset (listen first), moving through the mechanics (who to follow, how to curate), and arriving at the compounding effects of a well-maintained network over time.

## Chapter summaries

### Chapter 1: Listening Before Speaking
**Core argument:** Most professionals enter new learning communities by broadcasting before they understand the room. A listening-first posture accelerates trust-building and accelerates learning.
**Key evidence:** legitimate peripheral participation (EV-0003), listen-to-speak ratio (EV-0002), passive consumption rate (EV-0001).
**Transition:** Once you have listened long enough to understand the conversation, you are ready to choose who to follow.

### Chapter 2: Finding Your Network
**Core argument:** A personal learning network is built around a question, not a platform. Deliberate source selection produces better learning outcomes than algorithmic discovery.
**Key evidence:** curation satisfaction (EV-0007), weak-ties novelty (EV-0008), Dunbar limit (EV-0006), source diversity retention (EV-0009).
**Transition:** You have a list. Now you need a practice for working it.

### Chapter 3: Your Curation Practice
