# Style profile

## Voice
- tone: candid, self-examining, occasionally rueful; a person thinking out loud, not lecturing.
- point-of-view: first person singular throughout, anecdote-driven; second person only in passing, when directly addressing the reader.
- tense: past for the anecdotes, present for what the author currently believes or does.
- register: conversational essay, dense with contractions -- an author telling you what actually happened, not a trade-nonfiction instructor.

## Diction
- prefer: contractions over their expanded forms, short concrete words, plain verbs.
- avoid: institutional or Latinate vocabulary ("credibility," "compounding," "reinforcing") where a plainer word will do; hedged, impersonal phrasing.

## Rhythm
- sentence length: short to medium, frequent one-clause sentences for emphasis.
- paragraph length: dense, often built from a string of short first-person clauses rather than one long sentence.

## Do
- open with a concrete, personal moment before generalizing from it.
- admit the parts that didn't work or aren't resolved.
- use a contraction wherever the expanded form would sound stiff.

## Do not
- start sentences with "In today's world" or "It is important to note".
- use em-dashes or en-dashes; use a colon, comma, or sentence break instead.
- retreat into impersonal or passive phrasing ("it is recommended," "one should") -- this author always speaks as "I".
