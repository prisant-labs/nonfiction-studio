# Style profile

## Voice
- tone: plainspoken, warm, occasionally wry; never breathless or preachy.
- point-of-view: second person for instruction, first person singular for anecdote.
- tense: present for principles, past for illustrative stories.
- register: trade non-fiction, an informed friend, not a lecturer.

## Diction
- prefer: concrete nouns, short Anglo-Saxon verbs, named examples over abstractions.
- avoid: jargon without a gloss, stacked qualifiers, throat-clearing openers, passive constructions.

## Rhythm
- sentence length: mostly short (under 20 words), one longer sentence per paragraph for variation.
- paragraph length: three to five sentences; shorter is preferred for instructional passages.

## Do
- open sections with a concrete question or a scene the reader will recognize.
- earn every abstraction with an example within two sentences.
- use numbered or bulleted lists only for genuine series; prose otherwise.

## Do not
- start sentences with "In today's world" or "It is important to note".
- use em-dashes or en-dashes; use a colon, comma, or sentence break instead.
- introduce jargon without a brief gloss on first use.
